A house, reconstructed from a walkaround

10 mins to capture - High quality visuals for auditing - All from a common android smartphone.

Jai Tyagi · 9 October 2026 · 4 min read

The aim was to create a high-fidelity 3D model of a house using only a smartphone, one that could be used for auditing and for drawing up floor plans quickly and efficiently. To accomplish this, a combination of photogrammetry and 3D reconstruction techniques were used. The result is a detailed, accurate representation of the house that can be explored interactively.

A quick breakdown of the pipeline:

  1. 1
    Data capture

    Capture a series of images and videos of the house on a smartphone by walking around the property along a planned path. Through trial and error, I found that a spiral path captures detail most effectively without wasting time.

  2. 2
    Structure-from-Motion (SfM)

    The captured footage is processed with photogrammetry software, which analyzes the images and extracts key features to build a 3D point cloud.

  3. 3
    3D reconstruction

    The point cloud is used to build a detailed 3D model of the house, reconstructing its geometry and textures into a realistic representation.

  4. 4
    Interactive visualization

    Finally, the model is prepared for interactive viewing, so anyone can explore it from any angle.

Floor Plans

Here the processed SfM point cloud is used to generate a geometric representation of the house's layout. The orange frames are called view frustums that depict where the camera was positioned during the capture. As such, they provide context for understanding the 3D model's perspective. A top view or an orthophoto of this layout helps visualize the space and floor plan more effectively.

High Quality visuals for auditing

Here the point cloud is further processed to generate a high-quality visual representation of the house using 3D Gaussian Splatting. The resulting visuals can be used for auditing purposes, allowing for a detailed examination of the property without the need for physical presence.

To get the above results, 10 minutes of video taking at 30 fps was used. From this, 2-3 of the sharpest frames per second were extracted for processing. The whole procedure from data processing till point cloud generation was completed in 1.5 hours. To get the gaussian splatting models an additional short training of 40 minutes was done.
